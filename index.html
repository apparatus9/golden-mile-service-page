The Service

What forty years of service actually teaches you

There is a version of this page that lists our certifications, drops a few logos, and sends you off to a contact form. We could write that page in an afternoon. But it wouldn't tell you the one thing that actually matters when you're deciding who to let into your home or your business: whether the people showing up genuinely care how the work turns out after they've driven away.

Cass Rehman has been in service for more than forty years. He came up through the trade the long way, working for other people, and somewhere in those early years he settled on a rule that has never been written down anywhere official and has never once been broken: you finish what you start, and you leave it better than the drawing. Four decades later, that rule is the whole operating manual here. Everything else — the training, the scheduling, the guarantee, the way our crews clean up before they leave — is just that one idea, expressed in detail.

Service is a word that has been worn smooth by overuse. Every company on earth claims it. The difference is that most companies treat service as the thing that happens around the work — the friendly voice, the follow-up email, the little card left in the van. We treat service as the work itself. The courtesy is nice. The courtesy is not the point. The point is that the job is done properly, the first time, by someone who would be embarrassed to do it any other way.

“You can't fake forty years. You either did the work right that whole time, or you're not still here.”

Cass Rehman, Founder

Service means answering the phone

It sounds almost too simple to say out loud. But ask anyone who has tried to get a straight answer out of a modern service company and you'll understand why we lead with it. When you call us, a person picks up. Not a menu. Not a chatbot that asks you to describe your problem in fewer than two hundred characters. A person, in our office, who knows the crews by name and can tell you honestly whether we can be there Thursday.

If we can't help you, we say so, and we usually know who can. We have sent a genuinely surprising amount of work to other outfits over the years, simply because it wasn't ours to do well. Cass's view has always been that a referral you didn't have to give is worth more than a job you shouldn't have taken. Forty years into a career, that arithmetic has proven itself many times over.

Service means telling you the truth about the price

Our quotes are the price. Not a starting point, not an optimistic sketch that grows once the walls are open. If we find something genuinely unforeseen — and in this line of work, occasionally we do — we stop, we show you, we explain the options in plain language, and we don't touch a tool until you've said yes. Nobody has ever been surprised by an invoice from this company, and we intend to keep that record clean.

We've lost work over this. Plenty of it. There will always be someone who quotes lower because they've priced the job for the best possible day rather than a realistic one. We'd rather lose the job than win it dishonestly, and we'd rather you hear that now than find out the expensive way later.

Service means the job isn't finished when the work is

Anyone can install something. The measure of a service company is what happens in month eleven, when something isn't sitting quite right and the person who did the work has long since moved on. We answer that call the same way we answered the first one. Our workmanship guarantee isn't a marketing document with an escape hatch in clause nine — it's Cass's name, and he has never been comfortable with his name on something that doesn't work.

That's why we still service work Cass put in decades ago. It's why the second and third generation of some client families still call and ask for him by name. And it's why we've never needed a sales team: a career's worth of making the follow-up call nobody asked for turns out to be the most effective marketing anyone has ever invented.

Service means respecting the space you work in

Drop cloths before tools. Shoe covers on, always, without being asked. Radios low. Every offcut, every wrapper, every screw swept up and taken with us. When we leave, the only evidence we were there should be the thing we came to do. This is not a premium add-on and it is not a line item in the quote. It is the baseline, because someone's home or business is not a worksite to them — it's their life, and we're guests in it for a day.

New crew members sometimes think the cleanup standard is excessive. They stop thinking that around the third time a client asks for them by name.

The Standard

How a job runs here, every single time

Four steps. Unchanged since the eighties, because they still work better than anything that's come along since.

STEP ONE

We listen first

Before we measure anything, we ask what you're actually trying to achieve. Half the bad work in this trade comes from solving the wrong problem beautifully.

STEP TWO

We quote honestly

Itemised, fixed, and explained line by line. If there's a cheaper route we'd genuinely recommend, it's written in there too.

STEP THREE

We do the work

One crew, one lead, one point of contact. You'll know where we are each day and what's happening tomorrow.

STEP FOUR

We stand behind it

Walkthrough at handover, paperwork in your hand, and a guarantee we honour without an argument. Then we call you in a month to check.

A Closing Thought

Why experience is the only thing that can't be faked

Every business in this trade can buy the same equipment. Most can hire capable hands. What nobody can shortcut is the fortieth year of watching how work behaves over time — which materials quietly fail, which clever solutions become somebody's expensive problem in a decade, which warning sign on a Tuesday morning means you stop and call the client before you go any further.

That is the real inventory of a company like ours. Not the vans. Not the certifications, though we have those and will show them to you. It's four decades of consequences, absorbed and remembered by one person who stayed in this trade his whole working life and passed what he learned to everyone who came after him.

“I tell new starters the same thing every time. The client isn't paying for your hours. They're paying for the fact that they get to stop worrying about it. That's the product.”

Cass Rehman

So when we say service, this is what we mean. Not a smile at the door, though you'll get one. Not a follow-up survey, which we've never sent and never will. We mean the quiet, boring, unglamorous discipline of doing every part of the job properly — including the parts nobody will ever inspect — for forty years running, because the alternative has never once seriously occurred to us.

If that's the kind of company you'd like working on your property, we'd be glad to hear from you.

Questions

The things people actually ask us

How much experience is behind the work?

Cass Rehman has worked in service for more than forty years — apprentice, tradesman, crew lead, and now the person who sets the standard here. He founded this company and has led it every day since, with no ownership changes, no mergers and no rebrands. Every senior crew lead on staff was trained by him personally.

Is Cass Rehman still involved day to day?

Very much so. He reviews and signs off every scope of work, trains each crew lead personally, and is still on site most weeks. If you'd like him to look at your job specifically, just ask — we'll usually say yes.

Do you charge for a quote?

No. The consultation, the walkthrough and the written quote are free and carry no obligation. If we think you don't need the work at all, we'll tell you that for free as well.

Can the price change once you've started?

Not without your written approval. Our quotes are fixed. If we uncover something genuinely unforeseen, we stop, show you, explain the options and wait. You will never receive an invoice you weren't expecting.

What does the guarantee actually cover?

Our workmanship, without a fine-print maze. If something we did isn't holding up as it should, we come back and put it right. Manufacturer warranties on materials run alongside ours, and we handle those claims on your behalf rather than handing you a phone number.

How quickly can you start?

Most assessments happen within a few days, and we'll give you a realistic start date rather than an optimistic one. Emergencies are handled the same day wherever we possibly can. We'd rather tell you honestly that we're three weeks out than promise Monday and disappoint you.

Are you licensed and insured?

Fully licensed, bonded and insured. We hand you the current documentation at the first visit without being asked — and we'd gently suggest you ask anyone who doesn't.

Do you take on small jobs?

Yes. Some of our longest client relationships started with a job that took an hour. We price small work fairly and we don't treat it as a nuisance.
